How much have house prices in Cardiff increased since 2000? What happened during the financial crisis, and how has the market performed in more recent years?

Using **HM Land Registry Price Paid Data**, we analysed annual average recorded sale prices and transaction volumes for Cardiff between 2000 and 2025.

The average recorded sale price increased from **£85,626 in 2000** to **£322,724 in 2025**.

That represents **276.90% nominal growth** across 25 years.

However, Cardiff's property market did not rise at a constant rate. The data reveals rapid appreciation, several negative years and changes in the number of completed transactions.

Our three original graphs explore these developments.

## How have Cardiff house prices changed since 2000?

The first graph shows the long-term trend in Cardiff's average recorded sale prices.

In 2000, the average was **£85,626**.

By 2007, the figure had reached **£186,169**, reflecting substantial appreciation during the early part of the period.

The average was **£184,762 in 2010**, following the disruption associated with the financial crisis.

By 2015, it had increased to **£205,121**.

Cardiff's average subsequently reached **£255,796 in 2020**, before increasing to **£322,724 in 2025**.

Over the full period, the cumulative nominal increase was **276.90%**.

The compound annual growth rate was **5.45%**.

This annualised rate is a mathematical summary of the change between the first and last years. It does not mean that Cardiff prices increased by the same percentage every year.

The chart demonstrates why examining the entire time series is more informative than comparing only two dates.

## Which years saw the strongest Cardiff house-price growth?

<img src="/images/article-45/cardiff-annual-house-price-growth.png" alt="Bar chart showing annual percentage growth in Cardiff average house prices from 2001 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

The second graph shows annual percentage changes in average recorded prices.

Cardiff's strongest year was **2002**, when the average increased by **22.65%**.

This occurred during the rapid appreciation of the early 2000s.

Annual growth percentages allow us to compare the rate of change across different periods.

A £10,000 increase represents a larger percentage change when the starting price is £100,000 than when it is £300,000.

However, annual changes in recorded average sale prices are affected by which properties happen to sell.

For example, a greater proportion of larger or more expensive homes changing hands can increase the average transaction price even without an equivalent rise in the underlying value of every property.

Therefore, this chart is not a constant-quality house-price index.

It is a description of recorded annual averages.

## How badly did the 2008 financial crisis affect Cardiff?

Cardiff's average recorded sale price was **£186,169 in 2007**.

The average then declined during the financial-crisis period.

The largest peak-to-trough decline in this annual-average series was **-5.76%**, between **2007 and 2009**.

The weakest individual year was **2008**, when the annual average fell **5.18%**.

These two statistics measure different things.

The annual change compares one year with the previous year, while drawdown measures the decline from an earlier peak.

The annual-average series recovered its previous nominal peak in **2013**.

This indicates that Cardiff's recovery took several years rather than occurring immediately.

Investors considering historical capital appreciation should pay attention to these periods.

An investment property may still require mortgage payments, insurance and maintenance while market prices remain below a previous peak.

## How much did Cardiff house prices rise between 2015 and 2025?

The average recorded sale price increased from **£205,121 in 2015** to **£322,724 in 2025**.

This represents **57.33%** nominal growth over ten years.

That figure is distinct from the much larger cumulative increase measured across the entire 2000–2025 period.

Choosing a different starting year can materially change the growth rate reported for a housing market.

For a property investor, this highlights the importance of avoiding overly selective historical comparisons.

A strong long-term result does not mean every purchaser experienced the same returns.

Purchase timing, financing and the type of property acquired all influence individual outcomes.

## What happened to Cardiff house prices between 2020 and 2025?

Cardiff's average recorded sale price was **£255,796 in 2020**.

By 2025, it had reached **£322,724**, representing **26.16%** growth.

The annual pattern is particularly interesting.

In 2021, Cardiff recorded growth of **10.75%**.

Growth was **5.41% in 2022** and **1.17% in 2023**.

The rate slowed to **0.82% in 2024**, before increasing to **5.95% in 2025**.

Unlike some cities that recorded declines in their annual average during 2023, Cardiff's figure remained positive.

However, the slowing growth rates during 2023 and 2024 illustrate how market momentum can change even when the annual average continues to rise.

The 2025 increase was also considerably stronger than Cardiff's 2024 increase.

These observations describe the dataset and should not be taken as forecasts of future appreciation.

## How many properties were sold in Cardiff?

<img src="/images/article-45/cardiff-annual-property-sales.png" alt="Bar chart showing annual recorded property transactions in Cardiff from 2000 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

The third graph examines annual recorded property sales.

Across the 26 years from 2000 to 2025, the dataset contains **143,986 Cardiff transactions**.

This is an average of approximately **5,538 transactions per year**.

The highest annual count was **8,414 in 2003**.

The lowest annual count was **3,952 in 2004**.

Transaction volumes provide information about market activity that average prices alone cannot reveal.

However, they are not a direct measure of demand.

A decline in completed sales could reflect multiple influences, including financing conditions, available supply or changes in the composition of the market.

The data does not identify the causes of individual annual changes.

## How did Cardiff transaction volumes change after 2007?

In 2007, Cardiff recorded **7,909 transactions**.

By 2010, the annual total had declined to **4,182**.

The fall in transaction activity coincided with the period following the financial crisis.

By 2015, recorded sales had increased to **5,609**.

The figure was **4,158 in 2020**, compared with **4,790 in 2025**.

These changes matter when interpreting average prices.

If the mix of properties sold changes substantially between years, the average transaction price may move for reasons other than changes in the value of comparable homes.

Analysing prices and sales together is therefore more informative than considering either metric in isolation.

## How volatile have Cardiff house prices been?

Of the 25 annual percentage changes, Cardiff recorded **21 positive years** and **4 negative years**.

The sample standard deviation of those annual growth rates was **6.24 percentage points**.

This provides a descriptive measure of historical variation.

The largest observed drawdown was **-5.76%**.

Neither statistic is a prediction of future price changes.

They also do not capture the financial risk of owning an individual investment property.

Mortgage leverage, repairs, void periods and unexpected expenses can materially influence an investor's financial position even when average local sale prices are increasing.

## Is Cardiff a good place to invest in property?

Cardiff's historical average sale prices show substantial nominal appreciation between 2000 and 2025.

However, strong historical capital growth does not automatically make a city a profitable place to purchase a buy-to-let property today.

An investor must assess the acquisition price, achievable rental income, borrowing costs, repairs, insurance and tax.

It is also important to examine local neighbourhoods and property types rather than relying on a single citywide average.

**Our dataset contains property sale prices and transaction volumes, not rents.**

It cannot establish rental yields, net cash flow or likely future investment returns.

Those questions require additional evidence and property-specific calculations.

## Data source and methodology

This article uses **HM Land Registry Price Paid Data**, grouped by the Town/City label **CARDIFF**, for the years 2000–2025 inclusive.

The average sale price is the arithmetic mean of recorded transaction prices in each year.

Annual growth measures the percentage change between consecutive annual averages.

Cumulative growth compares the average recorded prices in 2000 and 2025.

The compound annual growth rate uses the 25 elapsed years between those dates.

Annual growth volatility is the sample standard deviation of the 25 annual percentage changes.

Maximum drawdown measures the largest decline from a previous annual-average peak.

Transaction counts represent recorded completed sales.

**Important limitations:** Prices are nominal and have not been adjusted for inflation. Changes in the types of properties sold can influence annual averages. The Town/City label does not necessarily match a local-authority boundary.

The figures do not constitute a constant-quality house-price index and exclude rental income and ownership costs.

Historical performance does not guarantee future returns.
