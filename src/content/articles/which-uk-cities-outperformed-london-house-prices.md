London has long dominated discussions about British property investment. But has the capital actually delivered the strongest house-price growth in recent years?

We compared **20 cities in England and Wales** using HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, examining annual average recorded sale prices over four periods ending in 2025.

The striking finding is that **all 19 other cities outperformed London over both five and ten years** in this dataset.

This comparison uses nominal average transaction prices. It does not measure changes in the value of identical homes, rental returns or total investment performance.

## How many cities outperformed London?

| Period | London's total growth | Cities beating London |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5 years | +2.52% | 19 of 19 |
| 10 years | +23.39% | 19 of 19 |
| 15 years | +67.85% | 13 of 19 |
| 25 years | +273.53% | 11 of 19 |

The choice of starting year matters. London's relative performance looks very different over five years than over twenty-five.

## What about Coventry and the Midlands?

**Coventry:** Average transaction prices increased **20.17%** over five years and **265.18%** over twenty-five years. The twenty-five-year difference versus London was **-8.35 percentage points**.

**Birmingham:** Average transaction prices increased **19.33%** over five years and **268.69%** over twenty-five years. The twenty-five-year difference versus London was **-4.84 percentage points**.

**Leicester:** Average transaction prices increased **17.59%** over five years and **309.65%** over twenty-five years. The twenty-five-year difference versus London was **+36.11 percentage points**.

The Midlands comparisons demonstrate why it is useful to examine several periods rather than assume that one city has consistently been the strongest performer.

## What does this mean for property investors?

Historical capital growth is only one component of property investment performance. A city that outperforms London on average sale prices may still deliver a different result once rental income, financing, maintenance, taxes and selling costs are included.

Higher percentage growth from a lower starting price also does not necessarily mean a larger cash gain per property.

Investors should compare individual property types and neighbourhoods, not just broad city averages.

## Data source and methodology

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Geography:** 20 Town/City labels in England and Wales.
- **End year:** 2025.
- **Start years:** 2020, 2015, 2010 and 2000.
- **Growth:** (End-year average / start-year average − 1) × 100.
- **Outperformance:** City growth minus London growth, measured in percentage points.
- **Price measure:** Nominal arithmetic average of recorded transactions.

These are not inflation-adjusted figures or constant-quality house-price indices. Different mixes of properties sold can change the annual average independently of underlying property values.

Land Registry Town/City labels may not match administrative or metropolitan boundaries. Results should not be generalised to all UK cities, because the source covers England and Wales and this study selects only twenty locations.
