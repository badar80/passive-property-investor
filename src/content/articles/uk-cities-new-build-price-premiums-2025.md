Do buyers pay more for new-build homes in every city? Or are there places where existing properties sell for higher average prices?

We analysed **HM Land Registry Price Paid Data from 2000 to 2025** across 20 major cities in England and Wales. We separated new-build and existing transactions, calculated transaction-weighted average prices and compared the results.

**The headline result:** In 2025, **Bradford** had the highest observed new-build price difference among the eligible cities, at **+58.3%**. **York** had the lowest, at **+8.1%**.

These are differences between average recorded sale prices. They are **not** estimates of what an otherwise identical property would cost if newly built.

A higher city-wide new-build average could reflect a larger share of flats, detached homes, expensive developments or particular neighbourhoods. The city-level difference is therefore best treated as a descriptive market statistic.

## How have the premiums changed since 2000?

<img src="/images/article-27/city-new-build-premium-heatmap.png" alt="Heatmap showing annual new-build versus existing property average price differences across 20 cities from 2000 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

The historical heatmap reveals whether a city's observed premium has remained positive, turned negative or varied over time.

The chart contains **520 eligible city-year comparisons** out of a possible 520. A cell is blank when either category has fewer than 30 transactions.

This is important because new-build development can occur in bursts. A single large scheme may change both the volume and composition of new-build transactions in a particular year.

The figures are not adjusted for inflation, but each annual percentage compares new-build and existing-property prices within the same year.

## Does the result change by property type?

This third graph breaks each city's transactions into detached, semi-detached, terraced and flat/maisonette categories.

Comparing within property types reduces one important source of distortion: the possibility that new-build sales consist disproportionately of a different category of home.

However, even a new-build detached house and an existing detached house can differ greatly in size, postcode, plot area and specification.

Cells marked **n/a** have fewer than 20 transactions in at least one comparison group. We have deliberately avoided presenting those small-sample premiums as meaningful.

## What should property investors take from this?

An observed new-build premium is not necessarily an investment disadvantage, just as a new-build discount is not automatically a bargain.

For a buy-to-let purchase, investors need to compare the actual asking price with local comparable sales, achievable rent and the full cost of ownership.

New-build flats may have different service charges, lease terms and amenities from older flats. New-build houses may have different floor areas, energy performance and maintenance requirements.

It is also worth comparing completed developments with nearby resales rather than relying on a city-wide average.

## How we calculated the premiums

The premium is defined as:

**(Average new-build sale price ÷ average existing-property sale price − 1) × 100.**

A positive value means the new-build group had a higher average recorded price. A negative value means the reverse.

### Data and limitations

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Geography:** 20 named towns/cities in England and Wales, using the recorded Town/City field.
- **Years:** 2000–2025 inclusive.
- **Transactions:** Category A, positive sale prices.
- **Property types:** D, S, T and F.
- **New-build classification:** OldNew = Y; existing-property classification: OldNew = N.
- **Average:** Arithmetic mean of recorded transaction prices, weighted by transaction counts when combining property types.
- **2025 ranking threshold:** At least 100 sales in each new-build/existing group.
- **Historical chart threshold:** At least 30 sales in each group per city-year.
- **Property-type chart threshold:** At least 20 sales in each group per city-property-type combination.

Town/City labels are those recorded in the Land Registry dataset; they should not be interpreted as consistent administrative city boundaries.

The data does not control for property size, location within a city, condition, quality or changing transaction composition. The results are not causal estimates of the price effect of new construction.
