## Oxford House Prices: 2000–2025 Data Analysis

Explore Oxford average sold house prices, annual changes and long-term growth from 2000 to 2025. This analysis uses annual district-level transaction counts and arithmetic mean prices from the supplied filtered Land Registry export.

## Key findings

In **Oxford**, the mean recorded sale price was **£167,985 in 2000** and **£585,850 in 2025**. That is a **248.8%** change across the period. The 2025 annual mean was **-2.4%** relative to 2024. There were **1,414** qualifying transactions in the 2025 extract.

## Understanding Oxford property market cycles

Oxford's annual average sold prices increased substantially between 2000 and 2025, but the data shows several distinct phases rather than uninterrupted growth.

The figures are arithmetic means from qualifying transactions in the supplied Land Registry extract. They are not a repeat-sales index and do not measure the appreciation of identical properties.

### 2000–2007: Rapid growth before the financial crisis

Oxford's average recorded sale price increased from £167,985 in 2000 to £305,220 in 2007.

That represents approximately +81.7% growth over seven years.

The average reached £249,712 in 2003, £268,435 in 2005 and £305,220 in 2007.

The transaction count was 2,107 in 2000 and 2,376 in 2007.

However, volumes fluctuated considerably, including 1,258 in 2002 and 2,788 in 2006.

These changes matter because annual average prices depend partly on the types and values of properties sold in each year.

### 2008–2011: An unusual financial-crisis pattern

One distinctive feature of the Oxford series is that the average recorded price increased in 2008 rather than falling immediately.

The mean rose from £305,220 in 2007 to £332,304 in 2008, an increase of approximately +8.9%.

It then declined to £302,852 in 2009, a change of approximately -8.9%.

The average subsequently recovered to £342,165 in 2010 and £357,575 in 2011.

Transaction volumes fell from 2,376 in 2007 to 1,454 in 2008.

This represents a change of approximately -38.8%.

A smaller and potentially different mix of sales can influence the arithmetic mean, so the increase in 2008 should not be interpreted as proof that comparable Oxford properties appreciated during the financial crisis.

### 2012–2015: Another period of strong increases

The average recorded price increased from £375,939 in 2012 to £462,036 in 2015.

That represents approximately +22.9% growth over three years.

The annual mean reached £382,225 in 2013 and £424,504 in 2014.

By 2015, the average recorded sale price had exceeded £424,504 by £37,532.

Transaction counts were 1,501 in 2012 and 1,692 in 2015.

The price increases are substantial, but the dataset cannot establish how much resulted from changes in comparable-property values rather than the composition of recorded transactions.

### 2016–2019: Growth slows and reverses

Oxford's average recorded price increased from £483,962 in 2016 to £510,929 in 2018.

It then fell to £498,472 in 2019.

The change between 2018 and 2019 was approximately -2.4%.

Transaction volumes also declined, from 1,473 in 2016 to 1,190 in 2019.

This period contrasts with the more rapid average-price increases recorded earlier in the decade.

It also demonstrates why a long-term growth figure can conceal years in which the recorded mean stagnates or falls.

### 2020–2022: A sharp increase followed by slower growth

The average recorded price rose from £498,472 in 2019 to £556,887 in 2020.

That represents a one-year increase of approximately +11.7%.

The average subsequently reached £569,955 in 2021 and £571,147 in 2022.

Between 2020 and 2022, the mean increased by approximately +2.6%.

The transaction count was 1,082 in 2020, compared with 1,753 in 2021 and 1,452 in 2022.

The data shows how average recorded prices and transaction activity changed, but it does not separately measure the effects of financing conditions, buyer demand, inflation or shifts in the properties sold.

### 2023–2025: A peak followed by two annual declines

Oxford's average recorded sold price reached £611,928 in 2023.

This was the highest annual mean in the 2000–2025 series.

The average then declined to £600,248 in 2024 and £585,850 in 2025.

The change from the 2023 peak to 2025 was approximately -4.3%, equivalent to £26,078.

The 2025 annual change alone was approximately -2.4%.

Interestingly, transaction activity increased from 1,128 qualifying sales in 2023 to 1,414 in 2025.

That is approximately +25.4% more recorded transactions.

A falling annual mean alongside rising transaction volumes is not necessarily evidence that every Oxford property declined in value.

The composition of sales can affect the mean, and recent years may be revised as further transactions are registered.

## What Oxford's price history means for property investors

### Long-term price growth is not an investment return

The average recorded sale price increased from £167,985 in 2000 to £585,850 in 2025.

That represents approximately +248.8% nominal growth.

However, this figure is not the return earned by someone who purchased and held a particular Oxford property throughout the period.

An investor's actual return depends on the acquisition price, financing costs, maintenance, refurbishment, management fees, taxation, transaction costs and rental income.

The figures are also not adjusted for inflation.

### Purchase price and rental yield need separate analysis

Oxford's high average recorded sale price makes the relationship between purchase cost and rental income particularly relevant to prospective landlords.

Gross rental yield is calculated using annual rent divided by the property's purchase price.

The annual Land Registry extract does not contain rental data, so it cannot establish Oxford's gross or net rental yields.

Comparing Oxford with another city requires consistent evidence on both purchase prices and achievable rents for similar property types.

### Transaction volumes provide essential context

Oxford recorded 2,788 qualifying transactions in 2006, compared with 1,082 in 2020.

A smaller transaction sample can be more sensitive to the mix of flats, terraces, semi-detached and detached properties sold.

That is one reason the transaction-volume chart should be considered alongside the average-price and annual-change charts.

A district-wide arithmetic mean is useful for historical analysis, but it is not a valuation for an individual property or neighbourhood.

Before investing, buyers should investigate comparable local sales, rental demand, property condition and realistic operating costs.

## Methodology and limitations

- Source: user-supplied annual export derived from HM Land Registry Price Paid transactions.
- Geographic level: Land Registry district names as recorded in the export, not local authority boundary-normalised comparisons.
- Years: calendar years 2000 2025 inclusive.
- Filters applied when producing the supplied extract: `PPDCategoryType = A`, `PropertyType IN (D, S, T, F)`, with mean sale price calculated across included transactions.
- Average prices are arithmetic means, not medians or repeat-sales indices; no inflation adjustment.
- 2025 data may be affected by registration lag or later revisions.
- This is descriptive historical analysis, not an investment forecast or valuation.
