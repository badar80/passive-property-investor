Property investors often look for cities with strong long-term capital growth. But another question is worth asking: **which cities have experienced the most consistent annual increases in average house prices?**

We analysed HM Land Registry Price Paid Data for 20 cities in England and Wales between 2000 and 2025. Each city has 25 annual percentage changes, allowing us to compare positive-growth years, declines and uninterrupted growth streaks.

**Sheffield and Nottingham jointly led for positive years**, each recording **23 increases out of 25**.

However, **London had the longest uninterrupted growth streak**, lasting **17 years (2001–2017)**.

These measures describe annual average prices of properties sold, not the changing value of an identical home.

## Which cities had the most positive years?

<img src="/images/article-31/city-positive-house-price-growth-years.png" alt="Ranking of 20 cities by number of years with rising average house prices from 2001 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

A positive year is one in which the annual average recorded sale price exceeds the previous year's average. We count strictly positive changes, not flat years.

The median city recorded **21 positive years out of 25**.

## Which city had the longest growth streak?

<img src="/images/article-31/longest-city-house-price-growth-streaks.png" alt="Longest consecutive annual house-price growth streaks across 20 cities" loading="lazy" />

**London recorded 17 consecutive years** of increasing average prices, from 2001 through 2017.

The streak counts consecutive annual increases. It does not mean every property appreciated or that prices increased throughout every month of those years.

A long streak and a high total number of positive years are different measures. A city can have frequent growth interrupted by occasional declines, while another may enjoy one unusually long run.

## When did multiple cities experience declines?

<img src="/images/article-31/city-annual-house-price-growth-consistency-heatmap.png" alt="Heatmap showing annual increases and decreases in average prices for 20 cities, 2001 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

Green cells represent years of rising average prices, red cells represent declines, and grey cells indicate no change.

**2008 saw the most widespread declines**, with **18 of 20 cities** recording a lower average transaction price than the preceding year.

The heatmap makes common turning points visible, but it does not establish that every location or property type experienced the same movement.

## Does consistency produce higher returns?

The correlation between positive-growth years and annualised average-price growth was **+0.198** across these cities.

That is a weak positive relationship in this particular sample. More positive years did not reliably translate into substantially stronger annualised growth.

**Manchester had the highest annualised growth**, at **6.38%**, despite recording **22 positive years** rather than the maximum.

The correlation between positive years and the standard deviation of annual changes was **-0.061**.

These associations are descriptive, not evidence of causation. The sample contains only 20 cities.

## What should property investors consider?

Consistency in recorded average sale prices may be useful when researching a location, but it is only one part of investment analysis.

Investors should also examine purchase prices, rental demand, achievable yields, void periods, financing costs, repairs and the ease of selling.

A market with many positive years can still suffer meaningful individual declines. Likewise, a city with fewer positive years may achieve stronger cumulative growth if its increases are sufficiently large.

## Data source and methodology

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Geography:** 20 Town/City labels in England and Wales.
- **Price measure:** Annual arithmetic mean of recorded transaction prices.
- **Study period:** 2000–2025.
- **Annual changes:** 2001–2025, giving 25 observations.
- **Positive year:** Annual average price increased strictly relative to the previous year.
- **Longest streak:** Maximum consecutive positive annual changes.
- **Annualised growth:** (2025 average / 2000 average)^(1/25) − 1.
- **Volatility:** Sample standard deviation of the 25 annual percentage changes.

All prices are nominal and unadjusted for inflation. Average transaction prices are not constant-quality house-price indices.

Changes in the mix of property types, locations, sizes and transaction volumes may affect the results. The Town/City labels do not necessarily match administrative or metropolitan boundaries.

This analysis covers selected cities in England and Wales, not every UK city.
