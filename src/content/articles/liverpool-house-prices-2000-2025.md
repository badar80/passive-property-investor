How have Liverpool house prices changed over the last 25 years? The city's property market has experienced rapid appreciation, periods of stagnation and renewed growth. Understanding those cycles can provide useful historical context for property investors.

Using HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, we analysed annual average recorded sale prices in Liverpool from **2000 to 2025**. Our findings show substantial long-term nominal growth, but also demonstrate that property markets do not move upwards at a constant rate.

**Liverpool's average recorded sale price increased from £61,374 in 2000 to £239,652 in 2025**, representing **290.48% growth** over 25 years.

Between 2000 and 2005 alone, average prices increased from £61,374 to £128,546. That early surge is an important part of Liverpool's long-term growth story, and it illustrates why the choice of starting year can affect performance comparisons.

## How much have Liverpool house prices increased?

Across 2000–2025, the total nominal increase was **290.48%**, equivalent to a compound annual growth rate of **5.60%**.

Over the more recent decade, 2015–2025, average prices increased by **57.69%**. Between 2020 and 2025, the increase was **28.96%**.

Compound annual growth rate smooths the change between two endpoints into an equivalent yearly rate. It does not mean that the market actually achieved that return every year, or that an individual investor would have earned the same return.

## Liverpool's strongest and weakest years

<img src="/images/article-36/liverpool-annual-house-price-growth.png" alt="Annual percentage changes in Liverpool average house prices from 2001 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

The strongest annual increase occurred in **2004**, when Liverpool's average recorded price rose **28.56%**.

The weakest year was **2008**, with a change of **-3.40%**.

Of the 25 annual changes, **19 were positive** and **6 were negative**. This pattern is a reminder that even a market with substantial long-term growth can experience individual years of declining averages.

The standard deviation of annual percentage changes was **7.23 percentage points**. This measures the historical variation in annual growth rates, not the risk of a particular rental property.

## What happened to Liverpool house prices after 2007?

Liverpool's average recorded sale price reached **£142,699 in 2007** before entering a period of weaker performance.

By **2011**, the annual average had fallen to **£136,128**, a peak-to-trough decline of **4.61%**.

The previous annual-average peak was not exceeded until **2014**, a period of **7 years** from the original peak.

That extended recovery period matters because investors who need to sell during a downturn may not have the flexibility to wait for nominal prices to regain their earlier levels.

## Liverpool's historical house-price drawdowns

<img src="/images/article-36/liverpool-house-price-drawdowns-2000-2025.png" alt="Liverpool annual house-price drawdowns measured against previous annual-average peaks between 2000 and 2025" loading="lazy" />

A drawdown measures how far the current annual average price is below the highest average previously recorded in the dataset.

The graph displays zero when a new peak is reached and negative values when average prices are below a previous peak.

Liverpool's largest observed drawdown was **-4.61%**. Although that decline was comparatively modest in percentage terms, the recovery took several years.

Importantly, these figures are based on annual arithmetic means of sold properties. A change in the mix of properties transacted can affect the observed average, so the drawdown is not a like-for-like valuation loss for every Liverpool home.

## Liverpool house prices since 2020

In 2020, Liverpool's average recorded sale price was **£185,832**. By 2025 it had risen to **£239,652**, an increase of **28.96%**.

The data also show a decline of **2.24%** in 2023, followed by positive annual changes in 2024 and 2025.

The latest annual average is not necessarily the current market value of a particular property. Neighbourhood, property type, condition and transaction timing all matter.

## How many properties were sold in Liverpool?

The dataset contains **234,078 recorded sales** across Liverpool between 2000 and 2025.

The highest annual count was **14,926 in 2003**, while the lowest was **5,022 in 2009**.

Transaction volumes help provide context for annual price averages. When fewer properties sell, changes in the composition of those transactions can have a larger effect on the reported mean.

A high transaction count does not automatically indicate rising prices, and a low count does not necessarily indicate a falling market.

## Is Liverpool a good city for property investment?

Liverpool's historical figures demonstrate substantial nominal capital appreciation, but capital growth is only one component of a property investment decision.

A landlord also needs to consider achievable rent, rental demand, tenant turnover, maintenance costs, financing, taxes, insurance and periods without rental income.

Lower purchase prices may make a property more accessible, but that does not guarantee a stronger rental yield or higher net cash flow.

This analysis does not contain rental income data, so it cannot establish Liverpool's rental yields or the returns available on a leveraged buy-to-let investment.

## Data source and methodology

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Geography:** Liverpool Town/City label in the source dataset.
- **Coverage:** Annual recorded transactions, 2000–2025.
- **Average price:** Arithmetic mean of recorded sale prices.
- **Annual growth:** Percentage change between consecutive annual averages.
- **CAGR:** Annualised compound growth between period endpoints.
- **Drawdown:** Percentage below the preceding maximum annual average.
- **Sales volume:** Number of recorded transactions in each calendar year.

All prices are nominal and have not been adjusted for inflation. The averages are not constant-quality house-price indices. Changes in the property mix, transaction volumes and geographic coverage can influence the comparisons.

The Liverpool Town/City label should not automatically be treated as identical to the Liverpool local-authority boundary.

Historical price changes are not a forecast of future performance.
