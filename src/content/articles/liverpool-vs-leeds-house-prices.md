Liverpool and Leeds are two major northern English property markets, but their historical price performance has differed. Which city experienced stronger growth over the past 25 years, and which has consistently been more expensive?

We analysed HM Land Registry Price Paid Data for **2000–2025**, using annual average recorded sale prices for both cities.

**Liverpool recorded 290.48% nominal growth**, compared with **271.32% for Leeds**. However, Leeds had the higher average price in every year covered by the analysis.

## Liverpool vs Leeds: key house-price statistics

| Measure | Liverpool | Leeds |
|---|---:|---:|
| Average price, 2000 | £61,374 | £76,387 |
| Average price, 2015 | £151,978 | £182,357 |
| Average price, 2020 | £185,832 | £235,168 |
| Average price, 2025 | £239,652 | £283,640 |
| Growth, 2000–2025 | 290.48% | 271.32% |
| Growth, 2015–2025 | 57.69% | 55.54% |
| CAGR, 2000–2025 | 5.60% | 5.39% |
| Annual growth volatility | 7.23% | 6.32% |
| Largest observed drawdown | -4.61% | -7.00% |
| Recorded sales, 2000–2025 | 234,078 | 257,419 |

These figures represent arithmetic means of recorded sales. They do not measure the changing value of an identical property in either city.

## How have Liverpool and Leeds house prices changed?

In 2000, the average recorded sale price was **£61,374 in Liverpool** and **£76,387 in Leeds**.

By 2025, Liverpool's average had reached **£239,652**, while Leeds had reached **£283,640**.

Leeds was more expensive in **26 of 26 years**. Liverpool was more expensive in **0 years**.

The chart shows both cities experiencing substantial price appreciation, alongside periods of stagnation and temporary declines. The gap between their average prices was not constant throughout the period.

## Which city achieved stronger percentage growth?

<img src="/images/article-37/liverpool-vs-leeds-indexed-house-price-growth.png" alt="Indexed Liverpool and Leeds average house-price growth with both cities starting at 100 in 2000" loading="lazy" />

To compare percentage performance fairly, we indexed both cities' average prices to **100 in 2000**.

By 2025, Liverpool's index had reached **390.5**, compared with **371.3** for Leeds.

Liverpool's total growth was **290.48%**, versus **271.32%** in Leeds.

The compound annual growth rates were **5.60% for Liverpool** and **5.39% for Leeds**.

The indexed graph is particularly useful because Leeds started with a higher average price. It separates relative percentage appreciation from the absolute cost of buying a property.

## What happened between 2015 and 2025?

Liverpool's average price increased from **£151,978** to **£239,652**, a rise of **57.69%**.

Leeds increased from **£182,357** to **£283,640**, equivalent to **55.54%**.

Liverpool therefore also led on percentage growth over the most recent decade. However, the difference was narrower than over the full 25-year period.

This is why investment comparisons should consider multiple time horizons rather than selecting only the period with the most dramatic result.

## How much more expensive is Leeds than Liverpool?

<img src="/images/article-37/leeds-house-price-premium-over-liverpool.png" alt="Leeds percentage premium over Liverpool average recorded sale prices from 2000 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

In 2000, Leeds' average price was **24.46% above Liverpool's**, a difference of **£15,013**.

In 2025, Leeds' premium was **18.35%**, equivalent to **£43,987**.

The percentage premium narrowed between these two endpoints, even though the difference measured in pounds became larger.

This distinction matters when discussing affordability. A market can become relatively less expensive while its absolute price difference from another market continues to grow.

The chart also shows that Leeds' premium fluctuated substantially rather than following a straight line.

## Which city experienced more volatile house-price growth?

We measured volatility using the sample standard deviation of annual percentage changes in average recorded sale prices.

Liverpool's annual growth volatility was **7.23 percentage points**, compared with **6.32 percentage points** for Leeds.

Liverpool therefore experienced more variation in annual percentage growth over this period.

However, Leeds experienced a larger maximum observed drawdown: **-7.00%**, compared with **-4.61%** for Liverpool.

These measures capture different aspects of historical price behaviour. Greater annual volatility does not necessarily imply a larger peak-to-trough decline.

## How did the two cities recover after 2007?

Liverpool's largest observed drawdown ran from **2007 to 2011**. The previous peak was regained in **2014**.

Leeds' largest observed drawdown ran from **2007 to 2009**. Its previous peak was also regained in **2014**.

Although their trough years differed, both cities required several years to regain their earlier nominal average-price peaks.

For investors, this highlights the importance of financing resilience and the ability to hold property through weaker market periods.

## Do Liverpool and Leeds house prices move together?

The Pearson correlation between their annual percentage changes was **+0.780**.

This indicates a strong positive historical relationship between their annual price movements.

However, the cities did not experience identical growth rates in every year. Local market conditions and differences in the properties being sold can affect their annual averages.

Correlation is descriptive and does not prove that changes in one city cause changes in the other.

## Is Liverpool or Leeds better for property investment?

On the historical capital-growth measure used here, Liverpool performed better over both 2000–2025 and 2015–2025.

Liverpool also had a lower average recorded sale price in 2025, potentially reducing the capital needed for a comparable percentage deposit.

However, lower average purchase prices do not automatically mean better rental yields. Rental income, financing costs, maintenance, voids, taxes and local tenant demand must also be considered.

An investor buying in either city should analyse individual neighbourhoods, property types and realistic operating costs.

This dataset does not include rents or investment expenses, so it cannot establish which city offers higher net buy-to-let returns.

## Data source and methodology

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Geography:** Liverpool and Leeds Town/City labels.
- **Period:** 2000–2025 inclusive.
- **Prices:** Annual arithmetic mean of recorded sale prices.
- **Total growth:** Percentage change between endpoint averages.
- **CAGR:** Compound annual growth over elapsed years.
- **Volatility:** Sample standard deviation of annual growth rates.
- **Maximum drawdown:** Largest fall below a preceding annual-average peak.
- **Leeds premium:** (Leeds average / Liverpool average − 1) × 100.

All prices are nominal, not inflation-adjusted. The figures are not constant-quality price indices and may be influenced by the mix of homes sold.

Town/City labels are not necessarily equivalent to local-authority boundaries. Historical growth does not predict future investment returns.
