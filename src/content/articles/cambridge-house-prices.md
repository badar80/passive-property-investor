## Cambridge House Prices: 2000–2025 Data Analysis

Explore Cambridge average sold house prices, annual changes and long-term growth from 2000 to 2025. This analysis uses annual district-level transaction counts and arithmetic mean prices from the supplied filtered Land Registry export.

## Key findings

In **Cambridge**, the mean recorded sale price was **£152,635 in 2000** and **£595,244 in 2025**. That is a **290.0%** change across the period. The 2025 annual mean was **-0.4%** relative to 2024. There were **1,360** qualifying transactions in the 2025 extract.

## Understanding Cambridge property market cycles

Cambridge recorded substantial long-term growth in average sold prices between 2000 and 2025, but the annual figures reveal periods of rapid increases, relative stability and occasional declines.

The series is based on arithmetic mean prices from qualifying transactions in the supplied Land Registry extract. It does not measure the appreciation of identical properties over time.

### 2000–2007: Strong growth before the financial crisis

The average recorded sold price increased from £152,635 in 2000 to £299,856 in 2007.

That represents approximately +96.5% growth over seven years.

By 2003, the annual average had reached £222,937, before rising to £268,125 in 2006 and £299,856 in 2007.

The number of qualifying transactions was 2,013 in 2000 and 2,227 in 2007.

However, annual transaction volumes varied substantially, including 972 in 2002 and 2,364 in 2006.

These fluctuations matter because changes in the mix of properties sold can affect the average, even when the underlying value of comparable homes changes by a different amount.

### 2008–2011: Decline and recovery

The average recorded price declined from £299,856 in 2007 to £284,918 in 2008.

This was a change of approximately -5.0%.

The mean then increased slightly to £287,227 in 2009 before reaching £321,992 in 2010.

In 2011, it eased to £318,766.

Transaction activity was also lower than before the financial crisis, with 2,227 qualifying sales in 2007 compared with 1,383 in 2009.

That represents a reduction of approximately 37.9% in recorded transactions.

The recovery in the annual mean therefore occurred alongside a much smaller transaction sample than during the earlier expansion.

### 2012–2015: A particularly rapid increase

One of the most distinctive features of the Cambridge dataset is the movement between 2012 and 2015.

The average recorded price increased from £328,660 in 2012 to £486,740 in 2015.

That represents approximately +48.1% growth in just three years.

The annual averages were £365,684 in 2013 and £417,493 in 2014, showing substantial increases in successive years.

By 2015, the average recorded transaction price was approaching half a million pounds.

The transaction count was 1,460 in 2012 and 1,657 in 2015.

These figures establish a sharp increase in average prices, although they cannot isolate how much was attributable to comparable-property appreciation versus changes in the properties being sold.

### 2016–2019: Growth becomes less consistent

The average recorded price was £488,936 in 2016 and £524,059 in 2017.

It subsequently reached £528,144 in 2018 before falling to £516,546 in 2019.

The change from 2017 to 2019 was approximately -1.4%.

This contrasts with the rapid increases recorded between 2012 and 2015.

Transaction counts also declined from 1,644 in 2017 to 1,390 in 2019.

The figures suggest that the pace of growth in the arithmetic mean had slowed, but they should not be interpreted as a precise measure of like-for-like price changes.

### 2020–2022: Renewed increases

Cambridge's average recorded price increased from £520,050 in 2020 to £562,114 in 2022.

That represents approximately +8.1% growth over two years.

The annual average reached £553,349 in 2021 and £562,114 in 2022.

Recorded transaction counts were 1,222 in 2020, 1,721 in 2021 and 1,657 in 2022.

The data documents a rise in average prices, but it cannot separately quantify the influence of mortgage rates, buyer preferences, inflation or changes in the composition of sales.

### 2023–2025: High prices but little recent growth

The average recorded price increased to £586,063 in 2023 and £597,545 in 2024.

In 2025, however, the average declined slightly to £595,244.

The difference between 2024 and 2025 was £2,301, equivalent to approximately -0.4%.

Interestingly, recorded transaction volumes moved in the opposite direction.

There were 1,211 qualifying sales in 2024 and 1,360 in 2025, an increase of approximately +12.3%.

This is an important example of why price movements and transaction activity should be analysed together.

It does not necessarily mean that an individual Cambridge property lost value during 2025. The annual mean depends on which homes were sold.

The latest data may also be revised as further transactions are registered.

## What Cambridge's house price history means for investors

### A high average price does not establish investment performance

Cambridge's average recorded price increased from £152,635 in 2000 to £595,244 in 2025.

That represents approximately +290.0% nominal growth.

However, this is not the return earned by an investor who bought a specific property in 2000.

Actual investment performance depends on the purchase price, financing costs, repairs, maintenance, management fees, taxation, selling costs and any rental income received.

The figures are also not adjusted for inflation.

### Capital growth and rental yield are different questions

A relatively high average purchase price can make the relationship between rent and acquisition cost particularly important.

For example, gross rental yield requires an estimate of annual rent divided by the purchase price.

This dataset contains sale prices and transaction counts, not rents.

Consequently, it cannot establish whether Cambridge offers higher or lower rental yields than another city.

That question requires reliable rental evidence and a consistent comparison of property types and locations.

### Smaller transaction samples require care

Cambridge recorded 2,364 qualifying transactions in 2006, compared with 1,211 in 2024.

A change in the proportion of flats, terraces, semi-detached and detached properties sold can materially affect an arithmetic mean.

The district-wide average should therefore be used as historical context, not as a valuation for an individual house.

A property-level assessment still requires comparable sales, local rental evidence, condition checks and realistic estimates of ongoing expenditure.

## Methodology and limitations

- Source: user-supplied annual export derived from HM Land Registry Price Paid transactions.
- Geographic level: Land Registry district names as recorded in the export, not local authority boundary-normalised comparisons.
- Years: calendar years 2000 2025 inclusive.
- Filters applied when producing the supplied extract: `PPDCategoryType = A`, `PropertyType IN (D, S, T, F)`, with mean sale price calculated across included transactions.
- Average prices are arithmetic means, not medians or repeat-sales indices; no inflation adjustment.
- 2025 data may be affected by registration lag or later revisions.
- This is descriptive historical analysis, not an investment forecast or valuation.
