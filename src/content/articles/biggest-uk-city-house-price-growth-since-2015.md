Which UK cities have experienced the strongest house-price growth over the past decade? For property investors, the answer can challenge assumptions about where capital appreciation has been greatest.

We analysed HM Land Registry Price Paid Data for **20 selected cities in England and Wales**, comparing annual average recorded sale prices in 2015 and 2025.

**Manchester ranked first**, with **77.06% growth** and a compound annual growth rate of **5.88%**.

At the opposite end, **London recorded 23.39%** growth over the same period.

These are changes in nominal average transaction prices, not like-for-like property valuations.

The median increase was **52.88%**. All **20 cities** had higher nominal average prices in 2025 than in 2015.

## Manchester versus London: a surprising contrast

Manchester's average recorded sale price rose from **£158,540** in 2015 to **£280,715** in 2025.

London increased from **£653,481** to **£806,342**.

Manchester's percentage growth of **77.06%** was substantially higher than London's **23.39%**.

However, London recorded a larger absolute increase in average price: **£152,862**, compared with **£122,174** for Manchester.

This illustrates why percentage gains and pound-value increases should not be confused. Neither figure alone measures an investor's net return.

## Was growth stronger before or after 2020?

<img src="/images/article-34/uk-city-house-price-growth-two-five-year-periods.png" alt="Comparison of house-price growth in 2015–2020 and 2020–2025 for 20 cities" loading="lazy" />

Splitting the decade into two five-year periods reveals how growth patterns changed.

Manchester recorded **40.93%** growth from 2015 to 2020 and **25.63%** from 2020 to 2025.

London's corresponding figures were **20.36%** and **2.52%**.

The correlation between city growth in the two periods was **+0.242**.

That relatively weak positive relationship suggests the cities performing best in the first half did not necessarily lead the second half.

The two five-year percentages should not simply be added: cumulative growth compounds multiplicatively.

## Which cities moved up the price rankings?

<img src="/images/article-34/uk-city-house-price-rankings-2015-vs-2025.png" alt="Changes in the ranking of 20 cities by average sale price between 2015 and 2025" loading="lazy" />

Percentage growth does not always translate into a higher position in the price rankings.

**Manchester** made the largest upward move, from **17th** to **11th** most expensive in this sample.

**Newcastle upon Tyne** made the largest downward move, from **10th** to **17th**.

These are relative rankings within the same 20-city sample, not national league-table positions.

## Did cheaper cities grow faster?

The correlation between average 2015 prices and subsequent 2015–2025 percentage growth was **-0.742**.

This negative relationship indicates that cities with lower starting average prices tended to record stronger percentage increases within this particular sample.

However, correlation does not prove causation. Different local economies, housing mixes and market conditions may influence the figures.

## How did Coventry and Birmingham perform?

**Coventry** ranked **12th**, with **51.85%** growth between 2015 and 2025.

Its annual average sale price increased from **£177,762** to **£269,938**.

**Birmingham** ranked **9th**, with **54.03%** growth over the same period.

Birmingham's average increased from **£169,448** to **£261,000**.

Both cities recorded substantial nominal growth, but these city-wide averages conceal differences between postcodes, property types and neighbourhoods.

## What does this mean for property investors?

Capital growth is one part of property investment performance. Rental yield, mortgage interest, void periods, maintenance, tax and transaction costs also matter.

A city with strong historical price appreciation may not necessarily offer the best investment opportunity today.

Likewise, lower percentage growth does not automatically mean poor rental performance.

The figures are useful for identifying historical patterns and framing further research, not forecasting future returns.

## Data source and methodology

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Geography:** 20 selected Town/City labels in England and Wales.
- **Period:** Annual average recorded sale prices for 2015–2025.
- **Total growth:** (2025 average / 2015 average − 1) × 100.
- **CAGR:** ((2025 average / 2015 average)^(1/10) − 1) × 100.
- **Price rankings:** Relative positions within the same 20-city sample.
- **Correlations:** Pearson coefficients.

Prices are nominal arithmetic means of recorded transactions. They are not adjusted for inflation or changes in the mix of homes sold.

Town/City labels are not necessarily administrative boundaries, and this selection does not include every UK city.
