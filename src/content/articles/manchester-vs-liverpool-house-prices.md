Manchester and Liverpool are two of the North West's best-known property markets. Both attract interest from homebuyers and property investors, but which has delivered stronger historical house-price growth?

We analysed **HM Land Registry Price Paid Data** covering annual average recorded sale prices between **2000 and 2025**.

Manchester's average price increased by **369.38%** over the period, compared with **290.48%** for Liverpool.

Manchester therefore led on percentage growth over the full 25 years, although Liverpool remained the cheaper market in 2025.

## Manchester vs Liverpool: house prices in 2025

| Measure | Manchester | Liverpool |
|---|---:|---:|
| Average price, 2000 | £59,806 | £61,374 |
| Average price, 2015 | £158,540 | £151,978 |
| Average price, 2025 | £280,715 | £239,652 |
| Growth, 2000–2025 | 369.38% | 290.48% |
| Growth, 2015–2025 | 77.06% | 57.69% |
| CAGR, 2000–2025 | 6.38% | 5.60% |
| Annual growth volatility | 6.61% | 7.23% |
| Largest observed drawdown | -8.40% | -4.61% |

In 2025, Manchester's average recorded sale price was **£280,715**, compared with **£239,652** in Liverpool.

That is a difference of **£41,062**, equivalent to a **17.13%** Manchester premium relative to Liverpool.

These figures describe average transactions, not the price of an identical house in each city.

## How have Manchester and Liverpool house prices changed?

<img src="/images/article-35/manchester-vs-liverpool-average-house-prices.png" alt="Line chart comparing average recorded sale prices in Manchester and Liverpool between 2000 and 2025" loading="lazy" />

In 2000, Liverpool's average price of **£61,374** was slightly above Manchester's **£59,806**.

Both cities subsequently experienced substantial nominal price increases, but their trajectories were not identical. Manchester's average prices pulled further ahead particularly after 2015.

Across the 26 annual observations, Manchester was more expensive in **21 years**, while Liverpool was more expensive in **5 years**.

The graph also shows that house prices did not rise smoothly. Both markets experienced periods of slower growth and declines in their annual transaction averages.

## Which city delivered greater percentage growth?

<img src="/images/article-35/manchester-vs-liverpool-indexed-house-price-growth.png" alt="Indexed Manchester and Liverpool house-price growth with both cities set to 100 in 2000" loading="lazy" />

An indexed comparison sets both cities to **100 in 2000**. This removes their different starting prices and makes relative growth easier to compare.

By 2025, Manchester's index had reached **469.4**, compared with **390.5** for Liverpool.

Manchester's compound annual growth rate over 25 years was **6.38%**, against **5.60%** for Liverpool.

Compound annual growth rate is a smoothed measure across the full period. It does not mean prices increased by the same percentage every year.

## What happened between 2015 and 2025?

Manchester's average recorded sale price rose from **£158,540** in 2015 to **£280,715** in 2025, an increase of **77.06%**.

Liverpool's average rose from **£151,978** to **£239,652**, representing **57.69%** growth.

Manchester's stronger percentage increase over the decade is consistent with the longer-term comparison, but historical performance does not guarantee that the same pattern will continue.

## How did the Manchester–Liverpool price gap change?

<img src="/images/article-35/manchester-house-price-premium-over-liverpool.png" alt="Manchester average house-price premium relative to Liverpool from 2000 to 2025" loading="lazy" />

In 2000, Manchester's average price was **2.55% below Liverpool's**.

By 2015, Manchester was **4.32% more expensive, and by 2025 its premium had reached **17.13%**.

The highest annual premium in the dataset was **25.59%** in **2023**.

The lowest was **-3.11%** in **2009**, when Liverpool's average was higher.

A premium is not automatically evidence that one city's properties offer better quality or investment value. The mix of homes sold can differ substantially.

## Which housing market was more volatile?

We calculated the standard deviation of annual percentage changes in average recorded sale prices.

Liverpool's annual growth volatility was **7.23%**, compared with **6.61%** for Manchester.

Liverpool therefore had slightly greater variation in annual growth rates, despite Manchester having the larger maximum observed peak-to-trough decline.

Manchester's largest observed drawdown was **-8.40%**, from 2007 to 2009.

Liverpool's largest was **-4.61%**, from 2007 to 2011.

The correlation between their annual growth rates was **+0.836**, indicating that the two cities' annual average-price movements were strongly positively associated over this period.

Neither volatility nor maximum drawdown fully captures investment risk. Rental income, debt and liquidity are also important.

## Is Manchester or Liverpool better for property investment?

On this historical measure, Manchester recorded stronger capital-price growth over both 2000–2025 and 2015–2025.

Liverpool, meanwhile, had lower average recorded sale prices in 2025. A lower purchase price could affect the deposit and borrowing required, but it does not establish which city offers a better rental yield.

Investors should examine achievable rents, void periods, maintenance, local demand, mortgage costs and transaction taxes before comparing opportunities.

This dataset contains sale prices and sales counts, not rental income. It therefore cannot determine which city provides better cash flow or net investment returns.

## Data source and methodology

- **Source:** HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.
- **Coverage:** Manchester and Liverpool Town/City labels.
- **Period:** 2000–2025 inclusive.
- **Prices:** Annual arithmetic mean of recorded transactions.
- **Growth:** Percentage change between annual averages.
- **CAGR:** Compound annual growth rate across elapsed years.
- **Volatility:** Sample standard deviation of annual percentage changes.
- **Maximum drawdown:** Largest fall from a preceding annual-average peak.
- **Premium:** (Manchester average / Liverpool average − 1) × 100.

Prices are nominal and not adjusted for inflation. The averages are not constant-quality house-price indices: changes in the mix of properties sold can influence the results.

Town/City labels do not necessarily match local-authority or metropolitan boundaries. The analysis should not be interpreted as a valuation of identical homes.
